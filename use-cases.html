From the factory production line to the outlet shelves.

The AI and analytics use cases I own at OCTAVE, and my part in each.

01 · Production planning · Factory · Owned

Balancing product availability against working capital.

Production planning

A digital twin of the beverages factory. A model recommends the schedule for each production line so that no product runs out of stock, while keeping the stock that ties up working capital as low as it can be.

My part. I own it: the analysis, shaping the solution with the data science team, and solving the problems that come up between the model and the factory floor.

Problem, solution, value and my part →: Production planning
02 · PDP · Dispatch · Owned

Planned deliveries and lower transport cost.

Permanent Dispatch Planning

Gives each distributor fixed delivery days and splits the week's order across them, so trucks run to a plan instead of to whoever calls first.

My part. I measure how closely deliveries keep to each distributor's planned days, flag deliveries that fall off plan, and work out whether a miss was the order or the truck.

Problem, solution, value and my part →: Permanent Dispatch Planning
03 · DRS · Distributor · Owned

What to order, and when: data-driven ordering decisions for distributors.

Distributor Replenishment System

Recommends how much each ice cream distributor should order, product by product, for each delivery day. A weekly forecast sets the volume, and a daily refresh adjusts each order on the morning it is placed, using the latest stock and sales so distributors neither run short nor overfill their freezers.

My part. I compare what the model recommends with what distributors actually order, track stock against freezer capacity, run a feedback pilot that asks distributors why they changed an order, and test proposed rule changes by simulating them on past orders before they go to the business.

Problem, solution, value and my part →: Distributor Replenishment System
04 · ICDM · Distributor · Owned

Setting appropriate targets that balance incentive payout against incremental volume.

Ice Cream Distributor Margins

On top of their normal margin, ice cream distributors can earn monthly bonuses for hitting targets on sales, product mix, outlet coverage and range. This use case checks who was paid and whether it matched the rules, and whether the bonuses are actually changing behaviour. Because pay depends on beating a target, it also sets each distributor's monthly target with a model: past sales adjusted for seasonality, a fair share of the business unit's total, and a small stretch for distributors who usually beat target. When territories change, it re-maps history so no distributor is judged on sales that are no longer theirs.

My part. I built the monitoring that flags patterns suggesting a bonus is being gamed rather than earned: landing just above a threshold month after month, stopping at a cap, or buying stock that never sells on to shops. I also own the target model: the analysis behind each rule, the fixes when the numbers didn't add up, and checking its targets against the business unit's before they go out.

Problem, solution, value and my part →: Ice Cream Distributor Margins
05 · BevDM · Distributor · Owned

Linking margin to performance: balancing payout against the extra volume it brings.

Beverages Distributor Margins

Beverage distributors used to earn a mostly fixed margin, whether they pushed hard or not. Now part of it is paid only when they beat target, with an extra bonus tied to a second condition that can rotate to whichever measure needs lifting next. Targets are set by a model, so pay is earned rather than given away.

My part. I track who qualifies each month, watch for payments that reward catching up rather than real growth, and weigh the extra volume against what the bonus costs. The tracking feeds the weekly governance calls with the business.

Problem, solution, value and my part →: Beverages Distributor Margins
06 · Weather · Market · Owned

Using expected weather, favourable or unfavourable, to set fairer targets.

Weather and sales

Ice cream and drinks sell far more on hot, dry days. This puts each distributor's sales beside the weather in their own area, expected against actual, so a hit or a miss can be read as performance or as weather. It separates luck from effort, spots demand that wasn't captured, and points to fairer, weather-aware targets next.

My part. I own it: the analysis, turning weather and sales into a clear story for each distributor, and the problem solving that leads to the next phase.

Problem, solution, value and my part →: Weather and sales
