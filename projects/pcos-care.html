PCOS Care

Can machine learning help people identify potential PCOS risk earlier?

A research-led web application, built as my final year Computer Science project, that explores machine learning as a screening-support tool.

Role
Research, application development, machine learning experimentation, model evaluation and communication of prediction factors.
Technologies
Python, scikit-learn, Flask, Streamlit
Timeline
Final year, 2024 to 2025
Domain
Healthcare research

01

The problem

PCOS is a complex condition with varied symptoms. Identifying potential risk is hard, especially with limited access to structured screening.

02

Context and constraints

Many risk models produce opaque scores, and models that need full hormone panels do not help where those tests are not available.

03

My approach

  • Two pathways: an initial assessment from everyday signs and symptoms, and an enhanced assessment that adds hormone test results.
  • Chi-Square and ANOVA feature selection, SMOTE for class balance and an enhanced Random Forest.
  • Each result shows the factors that contributed to it.

04

Solution and architecture

A deployed web application with two screening pathways that explains each prediction. A screening-support tool, not a diagnostic system or a replacement for medical advice.

05

Implementation

  • Investigated the research problem and relevant literature.
  • Explored feature selection and machine learning approaches, and developed and evaluated the models.
  • Designed and built the web application and integrated the model outputs.
  • Communicated the research through an academic paper and conference publication.

06

Evaluation and validation

  • Stratified cross-validation, favouring recall, since a missed case costs more than a false alarm.
  • A structured usability study with real users.

07

Results and limitations

  • Published at IEEE ICAHS 2025.
  • Third place at the University of Westminster final year project showcase, 2025.
  • Extended paper selected for the MSIT Journal special edition, under review.

08

Lessons and next steps

In clinical machine learning, transparency is not a finishing touch. It is the condition for anyone adopting the tool.
